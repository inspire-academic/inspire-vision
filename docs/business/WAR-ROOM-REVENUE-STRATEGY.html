Revenue War Room — drafted 22 Aug 2026

Ten thousand a month,
by design.

Not a wish. A ladder. This is the plan for Inspire to generate £10,000/month — using what's actually real today, not what we hope to build — so it buys back the hours your 9-5 is currently taking from you physically, emotionally, and intellectually.

01

Where we actually stand

This isn't a plan built on hope. It's built on an audit of what's already real.

Assets already built and live

  • A fully working mentorship platform — onboarding, student dashboard, mentor portal, admin console, all wired to a live database. Not a prototype; real people are using it today.
  • Two genuine validated instruments, implemented item-for-item from the published research, not approximated: the EPOCH adolescent wellbeing measure and the Strength of Relationship scales (the same instrument Big Brothers Big Sisters uses, normed on 5,000+ real mentoring pairs).
  • A structured coaching methodology baked into the product itself — GROW-format session notes, REAL-framed goals, a mentor training gate that blocks approval until safeguarding and coaching competency is actually demonstrated.
  • A completed research synthesis ("Twelve Months") — five-agent research into what the world's best mentoring programs actually do, and why matches that end early leave a young person measurably worse off than never being matched at all.

The one honest constraint

The platform today is single-tenant — one deployment, one hardcoded admin list, one organization's data. It was never built to be sold to a second organization yet. That's not a wall — it's the first thing this plan works around, deliberately, without asking you to fund a multi-tenant rebuild before a single customer exists.

02

The gap the market actually leaves open

I looked at what churches and youth organisations currently pay for mentoring software, and what corporate mentoring platforms charge. There's a seat nobody is sitting in.

A

Faith-native tools today

thin

UK church mentoring resources are mostly free or nominal — schemes like Growing Young Disciples run around £200/year — but they're admin templates, not software: no validated instruments, no safeguarding-gated training, no data at all past the spreadsheet a volunteer keeps.

B

Corporate mentoring SaaS

wrong shape

Real depth exists here — Mentorloop runs $299–499/month, MentorCliQ $15,000–50,000/year — but it's priced and shaped for HR departments matching employees, not churches safeguarding minors. Nothing about it is faith-native, and the price point assumes a corporate budget a church youth group doesn't have.

C

The open seat

Inspire's

Evidence-based, safeguarding-first, faith-native, priced for a charity budget. Nobody else is building from validated adolescent-development research and shaping it around youth safeguarding and Christian formation at once. That's the actual, specific gap — and it's the one thing already built.

A single 100-participant, 12-month corporate mentoring program spends $20,000–26,000/year on software alone. What's already built here is worth more, to the right buyer, than what most youth organisations currently spend running a program a fraction as rigorous.

03

Three lines of attack

Not one big bet — three tracks running in parallel, weighted by how controllable and recurring each one actually is.

1

White-glove licensed deployments

~60% of target · recurring

Not self-serve SaaS — that's the multi-tenant rebuild we're deliberately not doing yet. Instead: a cloned, managed deployment of the real platform, stood up for one partner organisation or one network at a time, run by us. Priced as premium concierge, not commodity software, specifically because it's high-touch — their own branded instance, their own admin, their own data, someone answering the phone when something breaks.

Sold to networks, not individual churches where possible — a diocese, a denominational youth office, a national youth charity — one umbrella contract covering many affiliated groups, rather than closing thirty small deals one at a time.

2

The training curriculum, sold standalone

~25% of target · faster close

The five mentor-training modules already built — safeguarding basics, active listening, boundaries & consistency, GROW-style coaching, ask-don't-tell — are a real CPD course today, independent of the platform. Package and sell it directly to youth workers and small churches who aren't ready to adopt the whole system yet. Lower price, lower friction, much faster to close than a platform contract — and every buyer becomes a warm lead into Line 1.

3

Opportunistic, non-recurring

~15% of target · lumpy, by design

Small, fast grants (National Lottery Awards for All: £300–£10,000, quick turnaround) to fund a specific development sprint — never relied on as monthly income, since grant cash is lumpy and reporting-heavy. Alongside that: a paid workshop built from the "Twelve Months" research, sold to diocesan safeguarding officers and youth-network leaders as a one-off booking. Both exist to fund the work and open doors — neither is the thing that buys back your Tuesday evenings.

04

The AI mentor is the wedge

The mentor-prep-brief feature we already scoped — a short, generated briefing a mentor reads before each session, drawn from that mentee's actual GROW notes, goal progress, and wellbeing trend — isn't a fourth revenue line. It's the reason a partner org pays Inspire's price instead of a cheaper generic tool. It becomes the centerpiece of every sales demo, not a feature buried in a settings page. Build it before the first outreach email goes out, not after.

05

The 90-day sequence

Days 1–14

Productize

One clear one-pager for "Inspire Mentorship for Churches & Youth Networks." A demo instance. A named list of 20 real targets — dioceses, denominational youth offices, national youth charities. The AI mentor-prep-brief feature built and demo-ready.

Days 15–30

Outreach, not cold — pilots

Direct outreach to the 20 targets. Offer 3–5 of them a free 90-day pilot on a cloned instance, real mentors and mentees, in exchange for a testimonial and case-study rights. Pilots are how an unproven product becomes a reference customer.

Days 30–45

Cash bridge

Launch the training-course sales track to a parallel list of individual youth workers and smaller churches, generating early cash while the pilots are still running.

Days 45–75

Convert

Turn 2–3 strongest pilots into paid anchor contracts. Submit 1–2 small grant applications in parallel — low time cost, asynchronous, no dependency on the sales track.

Days 75–90

Reassess

Check the real numbers against the ladder below. Only once 3+ organisations are actually paying do we revisit whether real multi-tenant infrastructure earns its build time — the same "prove it's real before you generalize it" instinct already applied to the product itself, now applied to the business.

The honest caveat

Nothing here is failsafe — no revenue plan is, and I won't tell you otherwise. What this is: the highest-leverage path available given what's actually real about Inspire today, chosen because it depends on outreach and product — things within our control — rather than on donor sentiment or a grant panel's calendar. It still requires real selling, real conversations, real follow-up. The plan removes guesswork about where to point the effort. It doesn't remove the effort.
