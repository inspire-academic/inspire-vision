# Inspire Vision — Language Preservation Project Manual

### A guide for everyone helping preserve the Krobo and Dangme languages through *100 Voices of Krobo*

---

## About this manual

*100 Voices of Krobo* exists to help the Krobo language remain spoken, heard, learned and remembered across generations. That only works if real people — family members, teachers, traditional leaders, diaspora relatives, and volunteers — take part in building it.

This manual is a living, growing collection of simple, step-by-step guides for everyone involved in that work, whether or not you have any technical background. Each guide walks you through one specific task: how to contribute a recording, how to help review content, how to get a piece of the project live on the app. New guides will be added here over time as the project grows — think of this as the project's shared handbook.

You do not need to read the whole manual at once. Find the guide for the task in front of you, follow the steps, and reach out if anything is unclear.

## Guide 1: How to Upload Sound Recordings for the Alphabet Lessons

### What this guide covers

The Learn Klo alphabet lesson lets learners tap a letter and hear it spoken by a real Krobo voice. Right now, every letter is waiting for that real recording — we do not use computer-generated or invented voices anywhere in this project. This guide explains exactly how a set of real recordings gets from a phone or microphone into the live app.

### Why this matters

This project has one unbreakable rule: **we never invent Krobo words, spellings, or pronunciations.** If a letter's name or sound hasn't been confirmed by a real speaker yet, the app honestly shows "recording being prepared" instead of guessing. That means the alphabet lesson can only come alive one real recording at a time — and that starts with you.

### Before you record: what we need

For **each letter** in the alphabet (A, B, D, E, Ɛ, F, G, GB, H, I, J, K, KP, L, M, N, NG, NGM, NY, O, Ɔ, P, S, T, TS, U, V, W, Y, Z), we need **two short clips**:

| Clip | What it should contain |
|---|---|
| **Letter name** | The speaker says the *name* of the letter, clearly and naturally |
| **Letter sound** | The speaker demonstrates the *sound* the letter makes |

**Recording tips:**
- Record somewhere quiet, without background noise, wind, or echo
- Hold the microphone or phone a comfortable distance from the speaker's mouth
- Keep each clip short — just the name or sound, with a little silence before and after
- If you're not sure a recording came out clean, record it again — a second take costs nothing
- A phone's built-in voice recorder app is perfectly fine for this

### Step-by-step: from recording to the live app

**Step 1 — Record the sounds.**
Work with a native Krobo speaker to record the "letter name" and "letter sound" clips described above. It's completely fine to do this one letter at a time, or a few letters at once — you don't need all 30 before sending anything.

**Step 2 — Name your files clearly.**
Give each recording a simple, clear file name that says which letter and which type of clip it is — for example, files for the letter "A" might be named `A-name` and `A-sound`. This helps our team match every recording to the right letter without any guesswork.

**Step 3 — Send the recordings to the project team.**
Share the audio files with whoever is coordinating the technical side of the project (currently Eric). The files can be shared however is easiest — a shared folder, a message with attachments, or a USB/hand-off if you're local. There's no upload button in the app for this yet — it's a hands-on hand-off for now, which also means every recording gets a human look before it goes anywhere near the app.

**Step 4 — The team adds your recordings to the app.**
Once received, the recordings are added to the project and connected to the correct letters. This is done carefully, one letter at a time, so nothing gets mixed up.

**Step 5 — A native speaker or language reviewer confirms accuracy.**
Before a recording is marked as trustworthy in the app, someone with real authority on the Krobo language confirms that the name, sound, and spelling are correct. A recording existing is not the same as a recording being *approved* — this step protects the accuracy the whole project depends on.

**Step 6 — It goes live.**
Once approved, the recording appears in the alphabet lesson for every learner to hear — and we double-check it actually works properly on a phone before calling it done.

### What if I only have a few recordings, not all 30?

That's completely fine, and encouraged. Sending even one or two solid, clean recordings is real, valuable progress. The alphabet lesson can go live letter by letter as recordings come in — it does not need to wait for all 30 at once.

### Who to contact

If you're ready to send recordings, or you have questions about equipment, format, or anything else in this guide, reach out to the project coordinator before you start recording — a quick check-in up front can save a re-recording session later.
